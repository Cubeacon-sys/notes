\section{Reported Speech}

\subsection{Definition}

Reported speech is used to report what another person said without reproducing the speaker's exact words.

There are two forms of speech:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Direct Speech: }Reports the exact words of the speaker.
\item \textbf{Indirect Speech: }Reports the meaning of what the speaker said.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Direct Speech:}
\begin{quotation}
  Sarah said, ``I am studying English.''
\end{quotation}

In reported speech, quotation marks are removed, and changes may be made to
pronouns, verb tenses, and expressions of time and place.


\subsection{Changes in Verb Tense}

When the reporting verb is in the past tense, the verb in the reported clause
commonly undergoes \textit{backshift}. The tense is moved one step backward.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{Direct Speech} & \textbf{Reported Speech} \\ \hline
Present Simple & Past Simple \\
Present Continuous & Past Continuous \\
Present Perfect & Past Perfect \\
Past Simple & Past Perfect \\
will & would \\
can & could \\
may & might
\end{tabular}
\end{center}


\subsubsection{Present Simple $\rightarrow$ Past Simple}

\begin{quotation}
She said, ``I like English.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
She said that she liked English.
\end{quotation}

The general transformation is:

\[
\text{Present Simple} \rightarrow \text{Past Simple}
\]


\subsubsection{Present Continuous $\rightarrow$ Past Continuous}

\begin{quotation}
He said, ``I am studying.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
He said that he was studying.
\end{quotation}

The transformation is:

\[
\text{am/is/are + V-ing}
\rightarrow
\text{was/were + V-ing}
\]


\subsubsection{Present Perfect $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect}

\begin{quotation}
She said, ``I have finished my homework.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
She said that she had finished her homework.
\end{quotation}

The transformation is:

\[
\text{have/has + V3}
\rightarrow
\text{had + V3}
\]


\subsubsection{Past Simple $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect}

\begin{quotation}
He said, ``I went to Jakarta.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
He said that he had gone to Jakarta.
\end{quotation}

The transformation is:

\[
\text{Past Simple} \rightarrow \text{Past Perfect}
\]


\subsubsection{will $\rightarrow$ would}

\begin{quotation}
He said, ``I will help you.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
He said that he would help me.
\end{quotation}


\subsubsection{can $\rightarrow$ could}

\begin{quotation}
She said, ``I can swim.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
She said that she could swim.
\end{quotation}


\subsubsection{may $\rightarrow$ might}

\begin{quotation}
He said, ``I may come.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
He said that he might come.
\end{quotation}
