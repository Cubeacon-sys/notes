\subsection{Changes in Pronouns}

Pronouns must be changed according to their referents rather than according
to a fixed substitution rule.

\begin{quotation}
John said, ``I am tired.''
\end{quotation}

Since ``I'' refers to John:

\begin{quotation}
John said that he was tired.
\end{quotation}

Similarly:

\begin{quotation}
Lisa said to me, ``You are smart.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
Lisa told me that I was smart.
\end{quotation}

The correct pronoun therefore depends on who is speaking and who is being
addressed.


\subsection{Changes in Time and Place Expressions}

Expressions referring to time and place may change when the statement is
reported from a different context.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{Direct} & \textbf{Reported} \\ \hline
today & that day \\
tomorrow & the next day / the following day \\
yesterday & the day before / the previous day \\
now & then \\
tonight & that night \\
this & that \\
these & those \\
here & there \\
ago & before
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For example:

\begin{quotation}
She said, ``I will finish it tomorrow.''
\end{quotation}

becomes

\begin{quotation}
She said that she would finish it the next day.
\end{quotation}


\subsection{Say and Tell}

The verbs \textit{say} and \textit{tell} have different grammatical patterns.

\textbf{Say:}

\begin{quotation}
He said that he was tired.
\end{quotation}

\textbf{Tell:}

\begin{quotation}
He told me that he was tired.
\end{quotation}

The basic patterns are:

\[
\text{say + something}
\]

\[
\text{tell + person + something}
\]

Therefore:

\begin{quotation}
He said that he was tired.
\end{quotation}

and

\begin{quotation}
He told me that he was tired.
\end{quotation}

are correct, while ``He told that he was tired'' is incorrect.


\subsection{Reported Statements}

The basic structure is:

\[
\text{Subject + said/told + (that) + reported clause}
\]

For example:

\begin{quotation}
Direct: John said, ``I am busy.''

Reported: John said that he was busy.
\end{quotation}

The conjunction \textit{that} may be omitted:

\begin{quotation}
John said that he was busy.
\end{quotation}

\begin{quotation}
John said he was busy.
\end{quotation}

Both forms are grammatically acceptable.


\subsection{Reported Questions}

Reported questions use statement word order rather than normal question
word order.

\subsubsection{Yes/No Questions}

For questions that require a ``yes'' or ``no'' answer, use \textit{if} or
\textit{whether}.

\begin{quotation}
Direct: He asked, ``Are you tired?''

Reported: He asked me if I was tired.
\end{quotation}

The structure is:

\[
\text{Subject + asked + object + if/whether + statement}
\]

The auxiliary \textit{do} is not retained in the reported question.

\begin{quotation}
Direct: ``Do you like coffee?''

Reported: She asked me whether I liked coffee.
\end{quotation}


\subsubsection{WH-Questions}

For questions beginning with \textit{what, where, when, why, who, how},
or \textit{which}, the question word is retained.

\begin{quotation}
Direct: She asked, ``Where do you live?''

Reported: She asked me where I lived.
\end{quotation}

\begin{quotation}
Direct: He asked, ``Why did you leave?''

Reported: He asked me why I had left.
\end{quotation}

The essential rule is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Reported question = question word + statement word order}}
\]

Thus:

\begin{quotation}
She asked me where I lived.
\end{quotation}

is correct, while:

\begin{quotation}
She asked me where did I live.
\end{quotation}

is incorrect.


\subsection{Reported Commands and Requests}

Commands are commonly reported using:

\[
\text{tell + person + to + V1}
\]

For example:

\begin{quotation}
Direct: The teacher said, ``Open your book.''

Reported: The teacher told us to open our books.
\end{quotation}

Negative commands use:

\[
\text{tell + person + not to + V1}
\]

For example:

\begin{quotation}
Direct: Dad said, ``Don't touch that.''

Reported: Dad told me not to touch that.
\end{quotation}

Requests commonly use:

\[
\text{ask + person + to + V1}
\]

For example:

\begin{quotation}
Direct: She said, ``Please help me.''

Reported: She asked me to help her.
\end{quotation}
