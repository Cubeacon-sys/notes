\subsection{Multiplicity and End Behavior of Polynomials}

\begin{definition}[Multiplicity]
  The number of times a specific root or zero appears. If a factor $(x - r)$ is raised to a power $k$ in a factored polynomial, then the root $r$ has a multiplicity of $k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Multiplicity]
  \hfill \\
  $f(x) = 7x^2 (x - 2) (x + \frac{1}{2})^4 (x + 3)^5$ \\
  $f(x) = 7 \cdot (x)^2 \cdot (x - 2)^1 \cdot (x + \frac{1}{2})^4 \cdot (x + 3)^5 = 0$ \\ \\
  $x = 0 \quad \text{Even Multiplicity of 2}$ \\
  $x - 2 = 0 \to x = 2 \quad \text{Odd Multiplicity of 1}$ \\
  $x + \frac{1}{2} = 0 \to x = -\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{Even Multiplicity of 4}$ \\
  $x + 3 = 0 = \to x = -3 \quad \text{Odd Multiplicity of 5}$
\end{example}

\textbf{Note: }All roots/zeros/$x$-intercept intersect the $x$-axis but:
\begin{itemize}
\item Roots with even multiplicity will bounce upon the $x$-axis and not cross the $x$-axis.
\item Roots with odd multiplicity will corss the $x$-axis.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[End Behavior]
  Describes the direction of the graph as the input $(x)$ moves toward positive infinity $(+\infty)$ to negative infinity $(-\infty)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Multiplicity]
\item $f(x) = 3x^5 - 2x^2 + 1$ \\
  $f(x) \approx 3x^5$
\item $g(x) = -2x^3 - 5x^4 + 7$ \\
  $-5x^4 - 2x^3 + 7$ \\
  $f(x) \approx -5x^4$
\end{example}

\textbf{Note: }If a polynomial has a degree $n$ then there will be at most $n - 1$ ``turning points'' (local max/min).


All polynomials create a continouous curves with no ``cusps'' (sharp points).
